# IELTS Writing practice via sentence-by-sentence Vietnamese -> English translation, with AI feedback and adaptive difficulty

## Q1. What does research say about L1->L2 translation drills for writing (benefits, risks, when it helps, Vietnamese-specific errors)?

### Takeaway
L1->L2 translation is a strong "pushed output" task. Learners can't avoid structures they find hard, and it beat other form-focused tasks for remembering words and collocations in Laufer & Girsai (2008), though a replication found no extra advantage. It helps lower-proficiency writers most. Higher-level writers gain little and can make more meaning-blocking errors when translating. Vietnamese learners' typical errors come from L1 transfer: no articles, no tense or plural inflection, adjectives placed after nouns, "có" turned into "have", and word-for-word collocations. The drill should target these directly.

### Cited Findings
- Laufer & Girsai (2008, *Applied Linguistics* 29(4), 694-716). Contrastive analysis + translation (CAT) beat other form-focused tasks and meaning-focused tasks for short- and long-term retention of single words and collocations. Their reasoning: in L2 translation, the source text fixes the words the learner has to produce, so the learner can't dodge them — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/237961629_Form-focused_Instruction_in_Second_Language_Vocabulary_Learning_A_Case_for_Contrastive_Analysis_and_Translation); [ERIC replication](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1364952.pdf)
- A replication in an advanced Spanish course found that CAT and ordinary form-focused instruction both raised collocation knowledge significantly. The difference between the two was not significant, so the CAT advantage did not replicate — [Jensen, eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/9hs2k98v)
- A follow-up study tested machine translation + post-editing and sight translation as ways to learn collocations — [Springer 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42321-020-00059-x)
- Guy Cook (2010, *Translation in Language Teaching*) argues that 20th-century ELT "outlawed" translation without good reason. Translation builds awareness of how L1 and L2 differ. Its constraints stop learners from avoiding gaps and point their attention at hard L2 features — [arXiv 2403.03396 (cites Cook)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.03396); [ResearchGate review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/295540962_Translation_in_Language_Teaching_An_Argument_for_Reassessment)
- Vivian Cook (a different scholar) argues that the L1 is present in learners' minds whether teachers allow it or not, and that banning it only makes its use invisible — [Wikipedia: Multi-competence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-competence)
- Criticisms of translation: it teaches learners *about* language more than how to *use* it, and it can push out L2 use. A 2025 ELT Journal paper says translation saves time and builds confidence and clarity about meaning, but L1 use needs monitoring — [Academia review](https://www.academia.edu/40076323/Using_Translation_in_Language_Teaching_Exploring_Advantages_and_Disadvantages_from_Linguistic_Humanistic_and_Practical_Perspectives); [ELT Journal](https://academic.oup.com/eltj/article/80/1/174/7685009)
- Kobayashi & Rinnert (1992, *Language Learning* 42(2)), with 48 Japanese university students:
  - Lower-level writers benefited from writing in L1 and then translating (better content, organisation, style). Higher-level writers did not benefit much.
  - Translations were syntactically more complex.
  - Higher-level writers made more meaning-blocking errors when translating.
  - 77% preferred writing directly in English.
  - [Wiley abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-1770.1992.tb00707.x)
- In Cohen & Brooks-Carson (2001), two-thirds of students did better writing directly. Reviews put the conflicting results down to proficiency level — [ERIC EJ1308931](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1308931.pdf)
- Vietnamese error studies:
  - Le (2023), pre-intermediate students:
    - collocations 32.95%
    - word form 17.90%
    - prepositions 15.63%
    - subject-verb agreement 9.09%
    - sentence structure 9.09%
    - missing "to be" 8.52%
    - tense 3.98%
    - articles 2.84%
    - [IJTE](https://www.i-jte.org/index.php/journal/article/view/401)
  - Van Lang University argumentative essays: articles 13.18% and concord 11.82% were among the top errors. Its review cites an HCMC Open University study where tense errors were the most frequent (6,587 errors, about 57 per student) — [ERIC ED652232](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED652232.pdf)
  - Tra Vinh University: L1 interference errors in tense/aspect (85%), subject-verb agreement (100%) and noun plurals — [EAJournals](https://www.eajournals.org/wp-content/uploads/Interlingual-Errors-In-Vietnamese-English.pdf)
  - Vietnamese marks plurals with the particle "các/những" rather than an inflection, so learners drop the plural -s. Article errors are linked to Vietnamese having no articles — [AESS Vinh Univ.](https://archive.aessweb.com/index.php/5019/article/download/507/934); [TNU Journal](https://vjol.info.vn/tnu/en/article/download/104134/87743/)
  - Adjectives come after nouns in Vietnamese ("sách mới" vs "new book"). Translating word for word produces noun-phrase order errors — [Tay Do Univ. study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/360352792_SOME_COMMON_ERRORS_IN_VIETNAMESE-ENGLISH_TRANSLATION_OF_ENGLISH-MAJOR_JUNIORS_AT_TAY_DO_UNIVERSITY_VIETNAM); [Translation errors case study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/320676202_An_Analysis_of_Translation_Errors_A_Case_Study_of_Vietnamese_EFL_Students)
  - Existential "có" gets rendered as "have" instead of "there is/are". Students also produce two main verbs in a row and active/passive mismatches — [Da Nang Univ. JST 2018](https://jst-ud.vn/jst-ud/article/download/7370/5261/5893)
  - Because Vietnamese verbs don't mark tense or aspect, students struggle with simple vs progressive aspect, the copula "be" and adverb position — [Dao 2008, HPU TESOL WP](https://www.hpu.edu/research-publications/tesol-working-papers/2008-fall/6.2-05-Dao.pdf)
  - Literal collocations such as "uống thuốc" -> "drink medicine" (should be "take medicine") and dropped articles ("He puts book on table"). Collocation errors were 17.41% of grammatical errors — [TPLS 2025](https://tpls.academypublication.com/index.php/tpls/article/download/10137/8213/32237)

### Inferences
- Translation fits best as the core drill for roughly band 4.5-6.5 learners. At band 7+, shift toward "paraphrase / upgrade the model" and free writing, so learners don't become dependent on L1 and pick up word-for-word habits.
- The Vietnamese source sentences should be written naturally, not as calques of English, so learners have to restructure. Each sentence can be tagged with its "L1 trap" (e.g., "có" -> there is, an implicit plural, a needed article, tense from a time marker, adjective order, a collocation). That gives the AI a targeted check and the learner a hint.
- The Vietnamese error studies rank error types differently by level and task. Le (2023) found tense and article errors rare, while other studies found them frequent. Build error categories from the learner's own data rather than a fixed ranking.

### Gaps
- No study found on whole-IELTS-essay sentence-by-sentence translation drills specifically, or on back-translation (English model -> Vietnamese -> English) for Vietnamese learners.
- No source found on Vietnamese learners' run-on sentences or fragments (comma splices from Vietnamese clause chaining). Swan & Smith's *Learner English* is cited as a likely source but wasn't checked.

## Q2. How should sentence difficulty be adapted (ZPD, i+1, scaffolding/fading, mastery thresholds, simple rules, Elo/IRT-lite)?

### Takeaway
Aim for a high but imperfect success rate: Duolingo's stories keep about 90% known and 10% new, and theory points to about 15% errors. Remove support gradually and per learner (adaptive fading beats fixed fading). Track learner ability against item difficulty with a simple logistic (Elo/IRT-like) model, as Duolingo's Birdbrain does.

### Cited Findings
- Duolingo explicitly grounds difficulty in Vygotsky's ZPD and Krashen's i+1. Its Stories keep about 90% known vocabulary/grammar and 10% new — [Duolingo blog: right level of difficulty](https://blog.duolingo.com/right-level-of-difficulty/)
- Birdbrain estimates each exercise's difficulty and the learner's current ability, and updates both after every interaction. It uses an IRT-inspired logistic model to predict whether the learner will get the item right, then picks exercises in an optimal challenge zone — [Bicknell et al. 2023, "How Duolingo's AI Learns what you Need to Learn"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/369072543_How_Duolingo's_AI_Learns_what_you_Need_to_Learn_The_language-learning_app_tries_to_emulate_a_great_human_tutor); [Duolingo blog: LLM lessons](https://blog.duolingo.com/large-language-model-duolingo-lessons/)
- Duolingo now uses an LLM to generate exercises aimed at a target difficulty level, with human review — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/large-language-model-duolingo-lessons/)
- Research on combining knowledge tracing with controllable LM generation to produce exercises personalised to a target difficulty, tested on Duolingo data — [arXiv 2306.02457](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.02457)
- The "Eighty Five Percent Rule": for gradient-descent learners on two-choice tasks, learning is fastest at about a 15.87% error rate. The authors suggest that in humans it applies mainly to perceptual learning. Popular "aim for 85%" advice goes beyond what the paper derived — [Wilson et al. 2019, Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-12552-4); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2019/11/191105113457.htm)
- Expertise reversal effect: support that helps novices can hurt more experienced learners — [Kalyuga & Renkl 2010, Instructional Science](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11251-009-9102-0)
- Gradually fading worked-out steps beats switching abruptly from examples to problems (Renkl, Atkinson & Maier 2000; Atkinson, Renkl & Merrill 2003) — [Kalyuga & Renkl 2010](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11251-009-9102-0); [Wikipedia: Worked-example effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worked-example_effect)
- Adaptive (per-learner) fading gave better outcomes than fixed fading or plain problem solving in two Cognitive Tutor experiments — [Salden et al.](http://www.cee.uma.pt/ron/Salden%20et%20al.%20-%20The%20Expertise%20Reversal%20Effect%20and%20Worked%20Examples.pdf)
- When writing prompts for learning journals were faded adaptively based on each student's earlier performance, students outperformed those who kept the prompts (Nückles et al. 2010) — [Kalyuga & Renkl special issue](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/226130927_Expertise_reversal_effect_and_its_instructional_implications_Introduction_to_the_special_issue)
- IELTS grammar band targets that can serve as "levels" for model sentences:
  - Band 5 attempts complex sentences, but they are less accurate.
  - Band 6 uses a mix of simple and complex forms.
  - Band 7 uses a variety of complex structures and has frequent error-free sentences.
  - Band 8 has a wide range, and most sentences are error-free.
  - [IELTSMentor (public descriptors)](https://www.ieltsmentor.ai/answers/how-to-get-band-7-in-ielts-writing/); [Quizlet (older wording)](https://quizlet.com/246602138/ielts-writing-band-descriptors-grammatical-range-and-accuracy-flash-cards/)
- IELTS Liz on vocabulary range: band 5 minimum, band 6 adequate, band 7 sufficient, band 8 wide. Band 7+ candidates know when to paraphrase and when to keep words the same — [ieltsliz.com](https://ieltsliz.com/how-to-paraphrase-in-ielts/)

### Inferences
- **Proposed adaptive model (IRT-lite / Elo).** Not taken from any single source:
  - Each learner has ability θ on the IELTS band scale. Each sentence variant has difficulty b, starting at its target band.
  - P(success) = 1/(1+e^-(θ-b)).
  - After each graded first attempt, update θ += K·(score − P). Use K about 0.3 early and about 0.1 later. Optionally nudge b the other way.
  - Pick the next model-sentence band so that P(success) ≈ 0.75-0.85, roughly θ + 0.5 band, which is i+1.
- **Simpler rule-based fallback:**
  - Move up half a band after 3 consecutive first-try sentences scored ≥ target, or after a rolling success rate above 85% over the last 8.
  - Move down half a band after 2 consecutive misses, or a rolling rate below 60%.
  - Treat a sentence as "mastered" when it passes on the first try with no hints. A pass with hints counts partially, e.g. 0.5.
- Keep three versions of each Vietnamese sentence, one per band (e.g., 5.5 / 6.5 / 7.5+). Show the model one band above the learner's θ, not the band-9 version, which would be far outside the ZPD for a band-5 learner.
- Fade support per learner and per error category: if the learner keeps getting articles right, drop article hints first. Grade lower when hints were used.

### Gaps
- No published success-rate target found specifically for L2 sentence-production tasks. The 85% figure comes from binary classification theory, and the 90/10 split is for reading input, not output.
- No public details found on Birdbrain's exact parameters or update rates beyond "IRT-inspired logistic".

## Q3. What feedback works best per sentence; what does Cambridge Write & Improve do?

### Takeaway
Written corrective feedback does improve accuracy, but the size of the effect depends on proficiency and context. Direct correction tends to show larger effects than indirect, though the difference isn't significant. Write & Improve deliberately limits the amount of feedback, flags whole sentences it can't parse, gives no "right answer", and is built around revise-and-resubmit with a progress graph. LLM feedback sticks to the rubric well but is less accurate and prioritises less well than trained humans, especially on stronger texts.

### Cited Findings
- Kang & Han (2015, *Modern Language Journal*, meta-analysis of 21 studies): written corrective feedback has a substantive effect on L2 written accuracy, moderated by proficiency, setting and genre. The focused vs unfocused difference was not significant. Sources disagree on direct vs indirect: the best reading is that direct showed a larger effect size without significance — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/modl.12189); [ERIC review EJ1440791](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1440791.pdf)
- Ellis's (2009) taxonomy of feedback types: direct (gives the correct form), indirect (marks the error without correcting it), metalinguistic (explains the error). Truscott (2007) is the dissenting meta-analysis, finding a negative effect — [ERIC EJ1440791](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1440791.pdf)
- Write & Improve:
  - It is trained on the error-annotated Cambridge Learner Corpus (30M words).
  - It gives indirect, semi-corrective word-level feedback on spelling, grammar and vocabulary, plus a qualitative rating of each sentence.
  - If it isn't confident about what the learner meant, it flags the whole sentence as unusual instead of suggesting a fix.
  - It gives an estimated CEFR level and a personal progress graph per task.
  - It deliberately staggers feedback ("too much red pen is ultimately demotivating"). As learners fix errors, nearby errors get marked on the next view.
  - [W&I Help](https://help.writeandimprove.com/en/articles/1104369-how-does-write-improve-work)
- With Write & Improve, learners revise and resubmit as often as they like for new feedback and marks. Cambridge has released an annotated corpus of these revision cycles — [W&I Corpus 2024](https://researchdatasets.cambridge.org/datasets/write-and-improve-corpus-2024)
- Teachers report students resubmitting more than 10 times, aiming for 5/5 — [Wilkins review](https://michaelrwilkins.medium.com/review-of-write-and-improve-website-32e41bd631b5)
- Write & Improve's limits:
  - Its feedback is too shallow to help A2-B1 learners much — [App review](https://www.academia.edu/67802552/DIGITAL_APP_REVIEW_WRITE_AND_IMPROVE_CAMBRIDGE_ENGLISH_Digital_app_review_Write_and_improve_Cambridge_English)
  - The level it shows doesn't reliably reflect a learner's true level — [Atlas Language School](https://atlaslanguageschool.com/why-we-love-learning-website-write-and-improve/)
- Steiss et al. (2024, *Learning and Instruction* 91), 200 US grade 6-12 essays, not EFL learners:
  - Trained humans beat ChatGPT-3.5 on accuracy, prioritising, clear directions and supportive tone.
  - ChatGPT was better at referring to the rubric.
  - ChatGPT was most accurate on weak essays and less accurate on strong ones, and used a less supportive tone with weak essays.
  - [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381068991_Comparing_the_quality_of_human_and_ChatGPT_feedback_of_students'_writing); [Hechinger Report](https://hechingerreport.org/proof-points-writing-ai-feedback/)
- RCT with 150 Chinese university students: the ChatGPT-feedback group scored higher in writing than the AWE group and the control group, but their "ideal L2 writing self" went down — [T&F CALL 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09588221.2025.2454541)
- A trial with 459 German secondary students found GPT-3.5 feedback improved revision, motivation and positive affect — [via Hechinger/related summary](https://hechingerreport.org/proof-points-writing-ai-feedback/)
- Chatbot feedback improved writing as much as teacher feedback, but the gains held up less well over time — [Open Praxis](https://openpraxis.org/articles/10.55982/openpraxis.17.3.871)

### Inferences
- **Per-sentence feedback design:**
  1. A meaning check first: did the translation keep the Vietnamese meaning?
  2. Then up to 2-3 tagged errors on the first attempt, in the spirit of Write & Improve's staggering, using a fixed category set (article, plural, tense, SVA, word form, collocation, preposition, word order, there-is/have, run-on, punctuation, missing be).
  3. On the first attempt, give indirect feedback (highlight + category + short Vietnamese explanation).
  4. After the retry, give direct feedback: a minimal-edit correction of *the learner's own sentence*, shown as a diff.
  5. Last, an "upgrade" version one band higher that keeps the learner's structure where possible, plus the band model.
- Score each sentence on two separate axes: accuracy (errors) and range (band-level structures and vocabulary). Then a correct but simple sentence isn't marked "wrong", in line with the band 6 vs 7 descriptors.
- Allow several correct translations. Grade on meaning plus correctness, not string match against the model. Have the LLM say so explicitly when it is unsure (W&I's "unusual sentence" fallback) rather than inventing a fix.
- LLM graders are weaker on strong texts and on prioritising. Constrain them with a rubric, the list of L1 traps for the sentence, the band-tagged reference answers, a JSON output schema, and an instruction to rank errors by severity.

### Gaps
- No data found on the best number of retries per sentence, or on how much the "upgrade" version helps compared with minimal correction alone.
- The direct vs indirect question remains unsettled. Kang & Han's original effect sizes were not checked directly.

## Q4. How do Writing9, IELTS Liz, ielts-simon, IELTS Advantage structure writing practice?

### Takeaway
Most IELTS sites do essay-level checking or study of band-9 models. Sentence-level work exists mainly as Simon's "perfect sentence", gap-fill and correction drills, Liz's paraphrasing drills, and Chris Pell's "join simple sentences into complex ones". None found offers a Vietnamese-to-English sentence translation drill that adapts to the learner. That is a real gap the app can fill.

### Cited Findings
- **Writing9** (AI checker):
  - Paste a Task 1 or Task 2 essay and get an overall band plus the four criteria with sub-checks, e.g. GRA "mix of complex & simple sentences, clear and correct grammar".
  - It highlights problem phrases in the text with suggested corrections, and offers topic ideas, vocabulary helpers and progress tracking — [writing9.com/draft](https://writing9.com/draft); [writing9.com](https://writing9.com/); [IELTSbiz 2026](https://ieltsbiz.com/blog/best-ielts-writing-checker-tools)
  - The same essay's score can drift noticeably between submissions, so the reviewer advises watching the trend across many essays — [SmallTalk2Me 2026](https://smalltalk2.me/blog/tpost/ki8ben5e31-best-ai-ielts-writing-checkers-in-2026-a)
  - Price in 2026: about $7.99/week, $14.99/month or $29.99/year — [IELTSbiz](https://ieltsbiz.com/blog/best-ielts-writing-checker-tools)
- **ielts-simon:**
  - Read his band-9 model answer and list its "less common" vocabulary.
  - Build paragraphs from ideas: topic sentence -> "The main benefit is that..." -> "Another advantage..." -> "From a personal point of view...".
  - Task 1 lessons include "One perfect sentence", "Perfect sentence practice", "Correct the sentences", "Paraphrasing" and model essays turned into gap-fills.
  - [ielts-simon.study Task 1](https://ielts-simon.study/ielts-writing-task-1-academic/); [ielts-simon.study](https://ielts-simon.study/)
- **IELTS Liz:**
  - Band-9 model essays with examiner comments. About 280 words is enough, and well over 300 doesn't help.
  - Lessons on improving sentences for a higher score.
  - Paraphrasing drills: rewrite a paragraph with given words, and paraphrase the question for the introduction (background statement + thesis).
  - [ieltsliz Task 2 hub](https://ieltsliz.com/ielts-writing-task-2/); [Sample band 9](https://ieltsliz.com/ielts-sample-essay/); [Paraphrasing practice 2](https://ieltsliz.com/ielts-writing-task-2-paraphrasing-practice-2/); [Paraphrasing practice 3](https://ieltsliz.com/ielts-writing-task-2-paraphrasing-practice-no-3/)
- **IELTS Advantage (Chris Pell):** "complex" sentences are just two or more simple sentences joined with words like and/which/if. Use them when expanding a point or giving an example. Conditionals work well for examples. Compound sentences count too — [ieltsadvantage.com](https://www.ieltsadvantage.com/2015/03/27/ielts-writing-complex-sentence/)
- **Other prep sites:**
  - Complex structures also include complex noun phrases and nominalisation, needed for band 7+ — [IELTS Buddy](https://www.ieltsbuddy.com/complex-sentences.html)
  - It's better to attempt complex sentences and make mistakes, unless the mistakes impede understanding — [IELTS Charlie](https://ieltscharlie.com/ielts-grammar-tips/)

### Inferences
- **Reusable patterns for the app:**
  - Simon's paragraph frame (topic -> benefit -> another -> personal view) can give the Vietnamese essay its sentence-by-sentence structure.
  - Chris Pell's "combine simple sentences" can be a higher-band transformation step, e.g. "now join sentences 3+4 with *which*".
  - Liz's question paraphrase can be the first sentence of every Task 2 drill.
  - Writing9's four-criteria bar can be the essay-level summary after all sentences are done.
- Writing9's score drift suggests showing per-sentence scores and a smoothed essay band, and never presenting a single LLM band as definitive.

### Gaps
- IELTS Advantage's paid course structure wasn't checked, and the official IELTS band-descriptor PDF wasn't fetched (only secondary quotes). No competitor found offering an L1-translation IELTS drill; this is absence of evidence, not a thorough market scan. Vietnamese-market apps were not surveyed.

## Q5. Concrete UX recommendations (hint ladder, retry, show model after N tries, progress, spaced review)

### Takeaway
Combine three things: Write & Improve's revise-resubmit loop with limited, staggered feedback; a hint ladder that fades per learner (adaptive fading); and spaced review of the learner's own wrong sentences, scheduled by forgetting rate as in Duolingo's half-life regression.

### Cited Findings
- Write & Improve's model: limited feedback first, more errors revealed as earlier ones are fixed, unlimited resubmits, a progress graph per task — [W&I Help](https://help.writeandimprove.com/en/articles/1104369-how-does-write-improve-work)
- Adaptive fading of support beats fixed fading and abrupt removal. A tutor that gave hints instead of answers led to more learning — [Salden et al.](http://www.cee.uma.pt/ron/Salden%20et%20al.%20-%20The%20Expertise%20Reversal%20Effect%20and%20Worked%20Examples.pdf); [Kalyuga & Renkl 2010](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11251-009-9102-0)
- Half-life regression (Settles & Meeder 2016, ACL):
  - It estimates each item's memory half-life h = 2^(θ·x), and recall p = 2^(−Δ/h).
  - It roughly halved prediction error compared with Duolingo's previous algorithm and raised daily engagement by 12%.
  - The code is MIT-licensed.
  - [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/P16-1174/); [paper PDF](https://research.duolingo.com/papers/settles.acl16.pdf); [GitHub](https://github.com/duolingo/halflife-regression)
- Translation tasks focus attention on hard L2 features that learners would otherwise avoid — [arXiv 2403.03396](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.03396)
- ChatGPT feedback can lower learners' "ideal L2 writing self" even as scores improve, so tone and framing matter — [T&F 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09588221.2025.2454541)

### Inferences (design recommendations; synthesised, not from a single source)
- **Sentence loop:**
  1. Show the Vietnamese sentence, with its context paragraph greyed out.
  2. The learner writes a free attempt.
  3. The AI gives a meaning check plus up to 3 categorised highlights (indirect).
  4. Retry 1.
  5. The AI shows the remaining errors with metalinguistic hints in Vietnamese.
  6. Retry 2.
  7. Show the direct minimal-edit diff of the learner's sentence, then the "upgrade +1 band" version and the band model.
  8. Optionally, the learner retypes the model from memory: a quick output step that also gives spaced-retrieval data.
  - Reveal the model after 2 retries, or immediately on "give up". Never before the first attempt.
- **Hint ladder.** Each rung lowers the maximum score for the sentence, e.g. 100 / 85 / 70 / 55 / 40:
  - L0: no hint.
  - L1: grammar focus ("chú ý: thì + số nhiều", i.e. "watch out: tense + plurals").
  - L2: key collocation or word bank, shuffled, with distractors.
  - L3: first-letter hints for the key words (e.g. "t___ m______" for "take medicine").
  - L4: sentence frame with blanks.
  - L5: show the model.
  - Fade the default hint level per error category as that category's mastery rises.
- **Adaptivity:**
  - Track θ for the learner and per-category accuracy (articles, plurals, tense, etc.).
  - Choose the band of the model sentence to show at about θ + 0.5 band.
  - Raise or lower using the rules in Q2.
  - Show "Band mục tiêu câu này: 6.5" ("target band for this sentence: 6.5") so the learner knows the goal.
- **Progress:**
  - A per-essay graph of first-try score vs final score per sentence, in the spirit of W&I.
  - A per-category error trend.
  - An estimated band, smoothed over many sentences (because of the score drift seen on Writing9).
  - Celebrate fixing errors ("you fixed 4/5"), not just scores, to protect motivation.
- **Spaced review:**
  - Save every sentence that needed ≥1 retry or a hint level of L3+ as a review card: Vietnamese source -> learner reproduces it.
  - Schedule with a simple Leitner or half-life model, e.g. 1d / 3d / 7d / 21d, reset on fail.
  - Mix new sentences with ones that share the same error category (an "articles" mini-deck).
- **Proficiency gating:**
  - Below about band 5: shorter, simpler source sentences, plus a word bank by default.
  - Band 7 and above: switch the drill to "paraphrase/upgrade this English sentence" or free writing of the next sentence, given the evidence that translation helps advanced writers less.
- **Feedback tone:** keep explanations short, in Vietnamese, and constructive. Always give the reason for each correction, and don't show more than 3 issues at once.

### Gaps
- No controlled evidence found on the exact hint-ladder penalties, retry counts, or review intervals for L2 sentence translation. The values above are proposed defaults for A/B testing.
- No study found that measures IELTS band gains from sentence-translation drills specifically.
