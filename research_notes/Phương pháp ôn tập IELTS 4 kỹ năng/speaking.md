# IELTS Speaking: answer frameworks, practice methods, and personal idea-bank apps (research notes, Nov 2026)

## 1. Which answer frameworks are taught for each part, and what do the official band descriptors imply for length and structure?

### Takeaway
The test is short and fixed: 11–14 min. Part 1 is 4–5 min of short Q&A. Part 2 gives 1 min of prep, then a 1–2 min long turn. Part 3 is a 4–5 min abstract discussion. Teachers agree on small scaffolds:
- **Part 1:** answer + reason (+ detail/example), 2–4 sentences.
- **Part 2:** keyword notes, often arranged as Past–Present–Future.
- **Part 3:** opinion + reason + example (+ the other view), in variants called OREO, AREA, PREP and OJE.

The descriptors reward the ability to "produce long turns" with "flexible use of spoken discourse markers" (Band 7). They punish overused connectives (Band 5). So frames should be taught as flexible idea moves, not fixed phrase templates.

### Cited Findings
**Official test format**
- Total time is 11–14 min, face to face, recorded. It is the same for Academic and General Training — [British Council](https://takeielts.britishcouncil.org/what-is-ielts/how-it-works/test-format/speaking); [Cambridge English](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/ielts/test-format/)
- **Part 1 (4–5 min):** questions about yourself, home, family, studies, work and familiar interests — [British Council](https://takeielts.britishcouncil.org/what-is-ielts/how-it-works/test-format/speaking)
- **Part 2 (3–4 min including prep):** a task card, 1 min to plan notes, then 1–2 min of speaking. The examiner may ask one or two follow-up questions — [British Council](https://takeielts.britishcouncil.org/what-is-ielts/how-it-works/test-format/speaking); [Cambridge English](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/ielts/test-format/)
- **Part 3 (4–5 min):** issues linked to the Part 2 topic, "in a more general and abstract way" — [British Council](https://takeielts.britishcouncil.org/what-is-ielts/how-it-works/test-format/speaking)

**Band descriptors (official public version, ielts.org PDF)** — [IELTS Speaking Band Descriptors](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-guides/ielts-speaking-band-descriptors.pdf)
- **Fluency and coherence**
  - B7: "Able to keep going and readily produce long turns without noticeable effort"; "Flexible use of spoken discourse markers, connectives and cohesive features."
  - B6: "willingness to produce long turns"; discourse markers "not always appropriately."
  - B5: "Overuse of certain discourse markers, connectives"; "simpler language may be produced fluently."
  - B4: "repetitious use of connectives."
  - B8: "Topic development is coherent, appropriate and relevant."
  - B9: "Topic development is fully coherent and appropriately extended."
- **Lexical resource**
  - B7: "Some ability to use less common and idiomatic items and an awareness of style and collocation"; "Effective use of paraphrase."
  - B6: "Resource sufficient to discuss topics at length."
  - B8: "Skilful use of less common and idiomatic items."
- **Grammatical range and accuracy**
  - B7: "A range of structures flexibly used. Error-free sentences are frequent."
  - B6: "mix of short and complex sentence forms … limited flexibility."
- **Pronunciation**
  - B6: "Chunking is generally appropriate, but rhythm may be affected by a lack of stress-timing and/or a rapid speech rate."
  - B8: "Flexible use of stress and intonation across long utterances."
- **Memorisation appears only in the low bands:**
  - B3 (grammar): errors numerous "except in apparently memorised utterances."
  - B2 (lexis): "isolated words or memorised utterances."
  - B1 (grammar): "No rateable language unless memorised."
- **Notes on the descriptors:** a candidate "must fully fit the positive features" of a band, and is rated on "average performance across all parts of the test."

**Part 1 frames**
- Simon (ielts-simon.com): "make your part 1 answers short and simple. Just give a direct answer, followed by a reason or one extra piece of information." Negative answers are acceptable — [compiled Simon lessons via search summary](https://pdfcoffee.com/ielts-speaking-simon-1pdf-pdf-free.html)
- DC IELTS: ask yourself "why?" and add the reason with "because"; add examples with "To give you an example" — [DC IELTS](https://www.dcielts.com/how-to-extend-answers-in-part-1-speaking/)
- IELTS Studio uses answer → reason → detail. Under ~8 s is too short; past ~35 s is padding — [IELTS Studio](https://www.ieltsstudio.com/blog/ielts-speaking-part-1)
- IELTSMentor recommends 2–4 sentences — [IELTSMentor](https://www.ieltsmentor.ai/blog/ielts-speaking-part-1-questions-strategy/)

**Part 2 frames**
- British Council's official teaching worksheet uses the PPF (Past, Present, Future) strategy. It helps students speak at length and show a range of complex grammar. It is explicitly optional: "not essential … an aid" — [British Council PPF PDF](https://takeielts.britishcouncil.org/sites/default/files/speaking_part_2_ppf_strategy.pdf)
- IELTS Advantage uses 5 headings: introduction, past, description, opinion, future. It pairs them with grammar targets: used to / would for past habits, past perfect, present continuous, going to, future perfect — [IELTS Advantage](https://www.ieltsadvantage.com/2015/03/03/ielts-speaking-task-2-strategy/)
- **Using the prep minute:** about 10 s to pick a specific subject, about 40 s to write 1–2 keywords per bullet. Example notes: "swimming / age 7 / dad taught / lake near house / scared at first / useful now / confidence". Common mistake: reading notes aloud — [ieltsscore7](https://ieltsscore7.com/guides/ielts-speaking-part-2-cue-cards)
- The final "explain…" bullet usually invites the longest answer. Truth of the story is not assessed — [Global English](https://www.global-english.com/blog/ielts-speaking-part-2-cue-card/)
- **Coverage of the bullets (sources disagree):** Beacon says Part 2 is judged on speaking 1.5–2 min without breakdown, not on covering every bullet — [IELTS Beacon](https://ieltsbeacon.com/ielts-answers/speaking/cue-cards/). PrepLearnio says cover at least 3 of 4 — [PrepLearnio](https://preplearnio.com/guides/ielts-speaking-part2-strategy/)
- Note: STAR and 5W1H did not appear in the sources I fetched (see Gaps).

**Part 3 frames**
- **OREO** = Opinion, Reason, Example, Opposite view (or Extension), about 30–45 s — [IELTS Arena](https://www.ieltsarena.com/blog/ielts-speaking-part-3-questions); [TalkDrill](https://www.talkdrill.com/blog/ielts-speaking-part-3-questions-answers/). Variants: NIR Academy expands the last O to "Other view"; EZ Academy uses it to restate the opinion — [NIR Academy](https://niracademy.com/ielts-speaking-part-3-questions/)
- **AREA** = Answer, Reason, Example, Alternative (optional) — [IELTS-Master](https://ielts-master.com/the-area-technique-how-to-improve-your-ielts-speaking-part-3-score/). IELTS Prep Studio gives 45–90 s and says examples can be general, with no real statistics needed — [IELTS Prep Studio](https://www.ieltsprepstudio.com/speaking/part-3)
- **PREP** = Point, Reason, Explanation/Example/Experience, Point restated. Said to suit "why" questions, while OREO suits "Do you think…" — [properenglishgrammar](https://www.properenglishgrammar.com/speaking.html)
- **OJE** = Opinion, Justify, Example, plus a concession for higher bands — [IELTS Prep Studio](https://www.ieltsprepstudio.com/speaking/part-3)
- Keith Speaking Academy (ORE) advises avoiding personal examples in Part 3: "Think more generally and globally" — [Keith Speaking Academy](https://keithspeakingacademy.com/how-to-start-speaking-in-ielts-part-3/)
- **Simon's Part 3 structure:** idea → explain → (example), or firstly/secondly/finally. For "it depends" questions, explain each side — [IELTS Simon](https://www.ielts-simon.com/ielts-help-and-english-pr/2014/09/ielts-speaking-part-3-answer-structure.html)
- **Typical Part 3 length:** 4–6 sentences ([NIR Academy](https://niracademy.com/ielts-speaking-part-3-questions/)) or about 30–40 s ([TalkDrill](https://www.talkdrill.com/blog/ielts-speaking-part-3-questions-answers/))

### Inferences
- **Suggested answer-length targets for the app (prep-site consensus, not official):**
  - Part 1: 2–4 sentences (~15–30 s, ~30–60 words)
  - Part 2: 1.5–2 min (~200–280 words at a normal speaking rate)
  - Part 3: 4–6 sentences (~30–60 s, ~70–120 words)
- **Suggested frame templates (one per question type):**
  - **P1:** Trả lời thẳng → Lý do → Chi tiết/Ví dụ (direct answer → reason → detail/example)
  - **P2:** Notes grid with the cue-card bullets as rows and an optional PPF column, keywords only (max ~15 words in total)
  - **P3 opinion:** OREO
  - **P3 why/cause:** PREP
  - **P3 compare (past vs now, young vs old):** contrast + reason + example
  - **P3 future/speculate:** prediction + reason + hedge ("it's likely that…, although…")
  - **P3 "it depends":** side A + side B + own conclusion
- Band 5 penalises "overuse of certain discourse markers". So the frame should show *moves* ("give a reason") with a rotating pool of linkers, not one fixed opener ("Well, to be honest…") repeated in every answer.
- Band 6 vs 7 differs mainly in flexibility, paraphrase and less-common or idiomatic items. AI answers at a "target band" should differ in exactly these dimensions, not just in length.

### Gaps
- STAR and 5W1H as Part 2 frameworks did not come up in the sources I fetched. They are common in Vietnamese/Asian prep material but I have no citation, so treat them as optional variants.
- I found no official IELTS statement on ideal answer lengths in seconds. All length numbers come from prep sites.
- IELTS Liz's and e2language's own pages were not fetched directly.

## 2. Risks of memorised answers, and how to design so learners internalise ideas rather than scripts

### Takeaway
Examiners are trained to spot memorised answers. At best these get no credit; at worst they lower scores. The official descriptors tie "memorised utterances" to Bands 1–3.

Prep consensus is to prepare a few reusable stories and ideas (5–6 stories cover most Part 2 cards), not word-for-word scripts. Practise rebuilding them aloud in varied wording.

Research supports two practice techniques:
- Repeated, time-pressured retelling (4/3/2) for fluency
- Shadowing for pronunciation and prosody, with smaller, tentative fluency gains

### Cited Findings
**How examiners treat memorised answers**
- British Council: memorised answers "are very obvious" and examiners are trained to spot them — [British Council Voices](https://www.britishcouncil.org/voices-magazine/ten-donts-ielts-speaking-test) (seen via search summary; the direct fetch timed out)
- IELTS Liz, in a 2018 comment on her page (copy): examiners "won't accept a memorised answer". By asking across topics, the examiner learns the real level — [englishtest.info copy of ieltsliz page](https://englishtest.info/59479ade86.html)
- IELTS Studio: memorised chunks get no credit. The band comes from the spontaneous language around them. Examiners may ask an unexpected follow-up or change the angle to force spontaneous speech — [IELTS Studio](https://www.ieltsstudio.com/blog/ielts-speaking-part-1)
- **Warning signs** — [ielts-academic.com](https://ielts-academic.com/2011/11/29/ielts-speaking-10-common-mistakes-to/):
  - "written" style of English
  - unnatural intonation
  - rephrasing the question into one the candidate prepared for
- A memorised answer is scored down under Lexical resource and Grammar because it stops matching the question — [oneielts](https://oneielts.com/ielts/speaking/tips)
- **Disagreement on how the penalty works:** "no credit" (IELTS Studio) vs "penalty" (ielts-academic, oneielts). All sources agree there is no benefit.
- Official descriptors link memorised language to Bands 1–3 — [ielts.org PDF](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-guides/ielts-speaking-band-descriptors.pdf)

**Preparing stories and ideas instead of scripts**
- One good story can answer several cards ("Yes, and it is recommended"). "Five well-developed stories, one per category, beats fifty shallow ones" — [IELTS Studio](https://www.ieltsstudio.com/blog/ielts-speaking-topics)
- Cue cards fall into ~5 categories: People, Places, Objects, Experiences, Abstract — [iWorld Learning](https://www.iworldlearning.com/article/ielts-speaking-part-2-topic-categories-guide.html)
- PrepLearnio: write five ~200-word stories, each adaptable to 5–10 cards; at exam time ask "which story fits?" — [PrepLearnio](https://preplearnio.com/guides/ielts-speaking-part2-strategy/)
- **Practice drills for stories:** tell one story four ways, each stressing details that fit a different prompt. Answer three different cue cards with the same story, timed, out loud — [Verborise](https://verborise.com/ielts-speaking-part-2) / [IELTS Studio](https://www.ieltsstudio.com/blog/ielts-speaking-topics)
- Simon: "prepare good vocabulary for common topics", and prepare ideas for the main Part 2 topics (blog advice, via search summary) — [ielts-simon.study archive](https://ielts-simon.study/ielts-speaking/). His *Ideas for IELTS Topics* ebook is for Writing Task 2, not Speaking.
- IELTS Speaking Prep (a commercial app) tells learners to "practise rebuilding the idea aloud in slightly different language. This protects fluency when the examiner changes the wording" — [ieltsspeakingprep.com](https://ieltsspeakingprep.com/)
- IELTS AI Coach's FAQ warns against memorising generated answers. It recommends reading them only *after* your own first attempt — [search result summary; listed among answer-generator tools](https://ieltsai.app/ielts-speaking-topic-generator) (attribution to this exact URL is uncertain)

**4/3/2 and repeated retelling (research)**
- **Nation (1989):** the same talk is given in 4, then 3, then 2 min to new listeners. Fluency gains appeared by the third talk (fewer false starts, hesitations and repetitions). Fluency practice conditions: known language, focus on meaning, time pressure, large amount of output — [Gianfranco Conti summary](https://gianfrancoconti.com/2017/03/04/en-route-to-spoken-fluency-via-task-repetition-the-4-3-2-technique-and-market-place/); [TESL-EJ 2022](https://tesl-ej.org/wordpress/issues/volume26/ej102/ej102a1/)
- **De Jong & Perfetti (2011):** gains lasted and carried over to new tasks, through automatisation of longer chunks. **Saito et al. (2021):** 4/3/2 led to fluency but not accuracy, so delayed corrective feedback was added — [Saito et al. 2021 PDF](http://kazuyasaito.net/LTR2021.pdf)
- Nation lists "repeated retelling" as a fluency activity — [TESL-EJ](https://tesl-ej.org/wordpress/issues/volume26/ej102/ej102a1/)

**Shadowing (research)**
- **Systematic review of 44 studies (Whitworth & Rose, 2025):** shadowing "can help improve learners' comprehensibility, intelligibility, and accentedness" and suprasegmental control such as fluency and prosody. The authors call the evidence "tentative" — [Tandfonline](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/29984475.2025.2546827)
- **Individual studies:**
  - Indonesian Grade 11 class: significant fluency effect (η²=0.290) — [search summary of shadowing studies](https://my-senpai.com/insights/shadowing-language-learning.html)
  - A 13-week weekly study found minimal fluency effect — [CORE PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/pdf/236345595.pdf)
  - Shadowing is weaker for reducing accent — [WJEL](https://www.sciedupress.com/journal/index.php/wjel/article/download/27670/16932)

### Inferences
- **Recommended practice ladder for each saved answer** (scaffolds fade over time; in the spirit of 4/3/2, retelling and shadowing):
  1. Listen to the TTS of the learner's own AI answer and shadow it (prosody, chunking)
  2. Read aloud with ~30% of the content words blanked
  3. Speak from keyword notes only
  4. Answer from the question only
  5. Answer a *reworded or new* question that reuses the same idea, e.g. an AI-generated follow-up or a sibling cue card
  6. Repeat under shrinking time (e.g. P2: 2:00 → 1:40 → 1:20)
- Measure "internalisation" by idea coverage: did the learner hit the key ideas, in any wording. Do not use word-for-word match. Reward paraphrase; flag near-verbatim recitation as a "script risk".
- Store ideas and stories as the primary object and answers as derived views. One "story" (e.g. a trip to Đà Lạt) links to many P2 cards and P3 questions. This directly applies the 5–6 story strategy.
- Add accuracy feedback after fluency rounds (Saito 2021 found 4/3/2 alone does not improve accuracy).

### Gaps
- I found no official IDP/British Council quantification of how memorised segments are scored. Examiner training material appears to be confidential.
- I did not search the literature on spaced repetition or retrieval practice for spoken answers. The "fading text" and SRS recommendations above are inferences without sources.

## 3. How apps and sites structure Speaking practice, and which features matter

### Takeaway
The market splits into four groups:
- **Pronunciation coaches:** ELSA
- **Scoring engines/APIs:** Speechace
- **Full mock-test simulators with band estimates:** SmallTalk2Me and others
- **Question/answer banks:** IELTS Speaking Assistant, plus the forecast sites

A newer niche builds personal answer banks: you give your ideas or stories, AI writes answers at a chosen band. IELTS Speaking Prep and IELTS Speaking Lab are the closest analogues to the planned feature.

Topic lists rotate three times a year: Jan–Apr, May–Aug, Sep–Dec. Forecasts come from unofficial candidate reports and differ by site.

### Cited Findings
**ELSA Speak**
- Phoneme-level, colour-coded pronunciation scoring. An in-app Speech Analyzer records IELTS-style answers and maps the score to IELTS/TOEFL/CEFR/TOEIC. It tracks pace, hesitation and grammar — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/elsa-speak-english-learning/id1083804886); [aitools-directory](https://www.aitools-directory.com/tools/elsa-speak-speaking-pronunciation-coach/)
- Premium quarterly costs $59.99 — [aitools-directory](https://www.aitools-directory.com/tools/elsa-speak-speaking-pronunciation-coach/)
- Competitor reviews call it best for pronunciation drilling, "not for full IELTS Speaking band scoring". These reviewers are competitors, so possibly biased — [Sprechify](https://sprechify.com/blog/best-ai-ielts-speaking-apps-2026-honest-comparison); [IELTS Mentor AI](https://www.ieltsmentor.ai/guides/stimuler-vs-elsa-speak/)

**Speechace (API)**
- `score/speech` is for unscripted answers. It returns an overall IELTS 0–9 score plus subscores: fluency, pronunciation, grammar, vocabulary, coherence. `relevance_context` adds an on-topic check — [Speechace docs](https://api-docs.speechace.com/api-reference/score-speech); [IELTS questions guide](https://api-docs.speechace.com/solutions/test-prep-for-standardized-tests/ielts-speaking-questions)
- `score/text` is for read-aloud of a known text. It includes fluency metrics (rate, pauses, run length) and "fidelity detection" of off-script attempts — [Score Text](https://api-docs.speechace.com/api-reference/score-text)
- The vendor claims scores fall within 0.68 IELTS points of human examiners. v9.0 adds a Relevance subscore and score explainability — [Speechace](https://www.speechace.com/api-plans/); [v9.0](https://www.speechace.com/announcing-the-speechace-api-v9-0/)

**SmallTalk2Me**
- Simulates all 3 parts with time limits. Gives an estimated band on all four criteria, a transcript, grammar corrections and pronunciation insights, and tracks progress. Third-party directory descriptions; band accuracy is not independently verified — [stork.ai review](https://www.stork.ai/en/smalltalk2me); [Medium review](https://medium.com/@emmamillerw1990/smalltalk2me-review-ai-english-speaking-practice-app-e223f0c12edf)

**IELTS Speaking Assistant**
- A large bank of real questions, model answers and topic ideas. Added 2026 topics in January — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ielts-speaking-assistant/id1123576667); [ielts-assistant.com](https://ielts-assistant.com/)

**IELTS Speaking Prep** (closest analogue to the planned feature) — [ieltsspeakingprep.com](https://ieltsspeakingprep.com/)
- **Input:** 6 personal stories (a person; a place or experience; a challenge; a skill; something you love; an event), about 10 min of notes.
- **Gap-filling:** AI makes a "bold inference" to fill missing details, which the user reviews.
- **Assets:** the notes become "master stories, everyday details, and personal stances", reused across all answers.
- **Answer style per part:**
  - P1: "concise, direct responses with a personal reason"
  - P2: "developed story"
  - P3: "clear opinion, explanation and relevant example"
- **Band and output:** band options are 6.5 / 7.0 / 7.5. Every answer comes with a translation, and the whole quarter's set exports to Word.
- **Pricing:** $19.90–$29.90 per quarter, one-time.

**Other answer generators**
- IELTS Speaking Lab: pick a part, enter your ideas, choose level and detail, and get an answer plus a vocabulary list. Free plan gives 4 answers/day — [ieltsspeakinglab.com](https://www.ieltsspeakinglab.com/)
- Engnovate: free P1/P2/P3 generators with an optional "Ideas" field, aimed at band 9 — [Engnovate P1](https://engnovate.com/ielts-speaking-part-1-answer-generator/); [P2](https://engnovate.com/ielts-speaking-part-2-answer-generator/)

**Topic forecasts**
- Three topic windows a year. Sep–Dec is "the largest refresh of the year" — [Cathoven](https://resources.cathoven.com/ielts-speaking/topics-2026)
- September lists are the "thinnest and least reliable" — [SpeakShark](https://speakshark.com/blog/ielts-speaking-forecast-september-2026)
- **Sep–Dec 2026 Part 1 topics that several sites share:** Shoes, Paper/handwriting, Tiredness, Secondary school, Collecting things, Street markets, Fruit & vegetables, Social media, Politeness, Advertisements, Tidiness, Names — [ReadingIELTS](https://www.readingielts.com/ielts-speaking-part-1-from-september-to-december-2026); [IELTSActualTests](https://ieltsactualtests.com/speaking/2026-q3); [Scorefactory](https://en.scorefactory.io/ielts_speaking_forecast)
- **Lists conflict:** IELTSSpeakingLab lists different topics (Birthdays, Cars, Headphones, Jewelry…) — [IELTSSpeakingLab](https://www.ieltsspeakinglab.com/blog/ielts-speaking-part-1-topics-questions-answers)
- Fixed openers (Work/Study, Home, Hometown, Area you live in) stay every season — [ReadingIELTS](https://www.readingielts.com/ielts-speaking-part-1-from-september-to-december-2026)

### Inferences
- **Features that matter, in priority order for this app:**
  1. Question bank tagged by season (Jan–Apr / May–Aug / Sep–Dec) and part, with P2→P3 linking
  2. Record → transcript → band estimate per criterion
  3. Personal idea/story bank that generates answers
  4. AI follow-up questions (they mimic how examiners probe scripts)
  5. Rehearsal loop with fading scaffolds
  6. Pronunciation detail (lower priority; ELSA covers this niche)
- **Differentiator vs IELTS Speaking Prep and IELTS Speaking Lab:** competitors stop at "generate + export". The planned feature can add a built-in rehearsal loop with idea-coverage scoring and versioning.
- Forecast lists conflict. Label them "dự đoán, không chính thức" (forecast, unofficial) and let admins curate per season rather than auto-import.
- If using Speechace, `relevance_context` and fidelity detection could flag off-topic or recited answers.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch ELSA's, SmallTalk2Me's or Speechace's own marketing pages directly, and did not check pricing for Speechace or SmallTalk2Me Pro.
- There is no independent validation of any app's band-estimate accuracy. Speechace's 0.68 figure is a vendor claim.
- I could not confirm the exact rotation dates (whether the switch falls on the 1st of Jan/May/Sep).
- e2language and IDP practice pages were not reviewed.

## 4. Concrete UX recommendations: frame templates, "ý của tôi" → AI answer at target band, versioning, practice loop

### Takeaway
Build the feature around a learner-owned idea bank: stories, stances and everyday details, entered in Vietnamese or as keywords. AI turns these into band-targeted answers for each question, using the part's frame, and adds follow-up questions.

Every answer is versioned and goes into a rehearsal loop that hides text step by step. Success is judged by whether the learner covers their own ideas in their own words, not by verbatim recall. This follows both the anti-memorisation guidance and the 4/3/2 / retelling evidence.

### Cited Findings
- **Ideas → band-targeted answers with translation** is an existing, paid commercial pattern. The band "guides language preparation; it does not certify … or guarantee an exam score" — [ieltsspeakingprep.com](https://ieltsspeakingprep.com/)
- The same model exists with choices for level and detail, plus a vocabulary list — [ieltsspeakinglab.com](https://www.ieltsspeakinglab.com/)
- **Answer shape per part:**
  - P1: answer + reason (+ detail) — [IELTS Studio](https://www.ieltsstudio.com/blog/ielts-speaking-part-1)
  - P2: keyword notes + PPF — [British Council PPF](https://takeielts.britishcouncil.org/sites/default/files/speaking_part_2_ppf_strategy.pdf)
  - P3: OREO / AREA / PREP — [IELTS Arena](https://www.ieltsarena.com/blog/ielts-speaking-part-3-questions); [IELTS-Master](https://ielts-master.com/the-area-technique-how-to-improve-your-ielts-speaking-part-3-score/); [properenglishgrammar](https://www.properenglishgrammar.com/speaking.html)
- **Read generated answers only after your own attempt** — IELTS AI Coach FAQ (via search summary)
- **Examiners probe with follow-ups or a new angle to force spontaneous speech** — [IELTS Studio](https://www.ieltsstudio.com/blog/ielts-speaking-part-1)
- **Band-differentiating dimensions:** paraphrase, less common/idiomatic items, structure flexibility, discourse-marker flexibility — [ielts.org descriptors](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-guides/ielts-speaking-band-descriptors.pdf)
- **Fluency comes from repeated, time-pressured retelling** — [Nation 4/3/2 via TESL-EJ](https://tesl-ej.org/wordpress/issues/volume26/ej102/ej102a1/)

### Inferences (design proposal)
**Per-question screen (default state)**
- Question, part badge, season tag
- **Answer frame card:** 3–4 labelled slots, e.g. P1: *Trả lời · Lý do · Chi tiết* (answer · reason · detail). Each slot has a one-line Vietnamese hint and 3 rotating example linkers.
- **Default sample answer:** collapsed by default, with a "Thử trả lời trước" (try answering first) nudge before revealing it.

**"Ý của tôi" (my ideas) input**
- Per-slot fields in the frame (free text in Vietnamese, English keywords, or mixed), or one free box.
- Optional: link an existing Story or Stance from the learner's bank.
- Target band selector: 5.5 / 6.5 / 7.5.
- Toggle: "giữ đơn giản để dễ nói" (keep it simple so it's easy to say).

**AI output (structured JSON)**
- `answer_en`: spoken-style, contractions OK, length matched to the part
- `keywords`: 5–10 cue words for the fading drill
- `frame_mapping`: which sentence fills which slot
- `vocab_highlights`: 3–6 band-relevant collocations with Vietnamese glosses
- `translation_vi`
- `follow_ups`: 2–3 examiner-style probes, e.g. "Why?", "Has it changed?", a related P3 question
- `est_duration_sec`
- **Prompt rules:**
  - Do not invent facts beyond the learner's ideas without marking them as suggestions (IELTS Speaking Prep's "bold inference" is reviewable for the same reason).
  - Avoid written-style phrasing and stock openers.
  - Vary discourse markers across the learner's saved answers (Band 5 "overuse" risk).

**Data model**
- `idea_assets`: type ∈ story | stance | detail; Vietnamese and English text; tags
- `questions`: part, topic, season, `parent_p2_id` for P3
- `answers`: learner_id, question_id, `source_idea_ids[]`, target_band, version, `is_default_sample`, `created_by` (ai | user_edit)
- `answer_versions`: full history, diffable
- `practice_attempts`: answer_id, stage, audio_url, transcript, `idea_coverage_pct`, `verbatim_overlap_pct`, duration, band_estimate, `next_review_at`
- One story links to many questions. Show a "story coverage" map, e.g. "câu chuyện 'chuyến đi Đà Lạt' trả lời được 7 cue card" (the 'trip to Đà Lạt' story answers 7 cue cards).

**Rehearsal loop (each step gates the next)**
1. Shadow the TTS of the answer
2. Read aloud with fading text: 30% → 60% of content words hidden
3. Speak from keywords only
4. Speak from the question only
5. Answer an AI follow-up or reworded sibling question that reuses the same ideas
6. Timed shrink (4/3/2-style) for P2

**Scoring and scheduling**
- Score each attempt by idea coverage and length or fluency, not exact wording.
- If `verbatim_overlap_pct` is very high at stages 4–5, show a "nghe như học thuộc" (sounds memorised) warning and suggest rephrasing.
- Schedule reviews with spaced repetition on `next_review_at`.
- After the learner speaks, offer "Tạo phiên bản mới từ câu tôi vừa nói" (make a new version from what I just said). The AI polishes the learner's own transcript, so the saved answer moves toward their own voice.

**Versioning UX**
- Keep v1 (AI from ideas), v2 (user edit), v3 (polished from the learner's own transcript).
- Let the learner pin one version as "bản ôn" (the version to rehearse).
- Show the band delta between versions.

### Gaps
- I found no user research or efficacy data on idea-bank → AI-answer apps. The commercial products publish no learning-outcome evidence.
- The design proposal above is inference from the cited methods. It has not been validated with learners.
- Vietnamese-specific IELTS speaking app practices were not researched.
